Abstract
Background: Breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL) is
an emerging cancer that has been linked to the use of textured devices. Recent
increase in number and frequency of cases has led to worldwide regulatory action.
Objective: We aimed to longitudinally study the disease in Australia since the index
case was first reported in 2007.

p

risk. s

to 149 unique breast implants. The mean ange of patients was 48.2 years (range,
22.4-78.5 years). They had an average taime from implantation to diagnosis of 6.8
years. 51.7% of implants utilizedM in this cohort were Allergan Biocell devices. The
indication for implant usage was for primary cosmetic augmentation in 70%, post
breast cancer reconstruction in 23% and following weight loss/pregnancy in 7%. The

The development of a unique T cell anaplastic non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma related to
breast implants, breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL)
was first reported in 1997.1 In recent years, the number and frequency of diagnosed
cases appears to be increasing with over 700 confirmed cases2 and 33 deaths
reported globally.3 The epidemiology of BIA-ALCL shows some unique features with a
predilection for Caucasian women.2 There are only 3 known cases in women with
t
Asian descent and 1 case in a woman from African descent.2 The tu p mor presents in
i
most cases with an effusion in the space between breast implant and capsule
r
between 7-9 years following insertion of the implant.4 It ccan occur both following
breast implant surgery for breast cancer reconstrucstion or following cosmetic
u
augmentation. Whilst the majority of patients present in early stage disease, some
can present with solid tumor invading the nperi-implant capsule to subsequently
spread to adjacent tissue and draining laymph nodes.5 A number of countries have
reported their series and confirmMed a higher risk for textured breast implants.6-8
Textured implants were introduced as early as the late 1960s in an attempt to
provide stability and reduce encapsulation. The first texture to be introduced was
d
Polyurethane (Surgitek) followed by other technologies designed to alter the outer
e
silicone shell.9 There are a number of varying surface textures, classified by Jones et
t
al into 4 gradpes using measurement of surface area and surface roughness.10
Analysis beased on tally of confirmed cases and implant sales have shown that
implacnts with higher surface area and surface roughness confer significantly higher
rcisk to patients.6,11 A unifying hypothesis that BIA-ALCL is caused by a combination of
Atexture, contamination by bacteria and stimulation of genetically susceptible T cells
over time has been proposed for pathogenesis of this cancer.12,13 Other triggers of
chronic inflammation including particulates, friction and allergy have also been
proposed.2
We have previously published epidemiology of this disease in Australia and
New Zealand6,11 and now include the most updated case details and implant-specific
risk calculations for four distinct types of textured implant.

DISCUSSION
The latest analysis of risk of BIA-ALCL in the Australian population has confirmed that
d
implants with higher surface area/roughness carry higher risk. The majority (87.5%)
e
of patients are now being diagnosed in early stage through better awareness and
t
improved survpeillance. These patients are treated with surgical removal of implant
and capsuele alone, without the need for adjuvant therapy and have an excellent
progncosis.4
cThese updated risk data support the recent regulatory action to suspend or
Acancel devices that have higher-grade texture in order to reduce the forward
incidence of this cancer. The French regulator, the ANSM, was the first to suspend a
range of textured devices based on published and independent risk analysis for the
French cohort.18 This was followed by the FDA decision to initiate a recall of the
Biocell product, reversing their earlier decision, based on a rise in the number of
deaths related to this device.3 Allergan then chose to initiate a voluntary global recall
of their Biocell textured implants and tissue expanders.19 This action is supported by
our findings that the risk for Allergan Biocell (Grade 3) device has been steadily
increasing since the time of our first publication. The previous cancellation of the
Silimed PU device by regulators, albeit for other reasons,9 also ensures that the two

CONCLUSIONS
The risk of BIA-ALCL in Australia is rising and is significantly more associated with
breast implants of higher surface area/roughness. The levels of risk reported for
Australia justifies recent worldwide regulatory action. The training, experience and
certification of the practitioner using these devices and the facilities where these
t
p
procedures are performed may impact on the risk of BIA-ALCL and requires further
i
study. The use of sales data analysis in Australia has provided a clearer picture of the
r
emerging patterns of this disease and valuable risk vs. bencefit information to guide
clinicians, regulators and patients to ensure ongoing safsety and efficacy.
